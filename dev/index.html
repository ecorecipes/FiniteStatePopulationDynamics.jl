FiniteStatePopulationDynamics.jl

Finite-state, continuous-time population dynamics in Julia.

This package is the deterministic generator-based sibling of MatrixProjectionModels.jl: it takes the same discrete-stage state space and lifts it into continuous time via infinitesimal generator matrices and delay generator terms. Broad stochastic continuous-time semantics are intentionally deferred to future extension packages.

Resilience operators

linearise (from ResilienceCore.jl, re-exported here) turns a generator problem into the operator whose stability and transient metrics apply: a Generator for a constant generator, a ContinuousJacobian at the equilibrium when there is a constant source, a PeriodicOperator for a periodic generator, and the Jacobian at a given equilibrium for a density-dependent model.

using FiniteStatePopulationDynamics, ResilienceCore

G = [-0.5 0.1; 0.3 -0.2]
prob = FiniteStateGeneratorProblem(G, DiscreteDomain([:juvenile, :adult]), [1.0, 1.0], (0.0, 10.0);
                                   source = [0.2, 0.0])
J = linearise(prob)              # ContinuousJacobian at u* = −G⁻¹ b
resilience(J), reactivity(J)
ResilienceCore.linearise — Function
linearise(prob::FiniteStateGeneratorProblem, u = nothing; p = prob.p, t = first(prob.tspan),
          period = nothing, steps = 100, tol = 1e-8, check = true)

The resilience operator of a finite-state generator problem du/dt = G u + b (see ResilienceCore.jl), chosen by how G and the source b depend on the state and on time:

  • Constant G, no source: Generator(G), a density-independent model. Its growth_rate is the spectral abscissa of G; its resilience, Re λ₁ − Re λ₂, is the rate of convergence to the stable stage structure.
  • Constant G and constant source b (immigration, recruitment from outside): ContinuousJacobian(G, u*) at the equilibrium u* = −G⁻¹ b (affine_equilibrium). G must be stable.
  • G(t) periodic in time: pass period = T. The result is a PeriodicOperator of steps matrix exponentials over one period from t (periodic_generator), with per-step metrics per Δt = T / steps. A source does not change it: perturbations of any solution evolve under G(t) alone.
  • G or b depending on the state (density dependence): pass the equilibrium u. The problem is lowered with to_ode_problem and linearised there, giving a ContinuousJacobian; this needs ForwardDiff to be loaded. tol and check are passed on.
  • normalize = true (proportions): without a source, still Generator(G). The normalised dynamics do not grow, and their rate of convergence to the stable structure is resilience(Generator(G)). With a source the normalised model is non-linear and takes the state-dependent route.

A time-dependent term without period throws an ArgumentError. u is used only by the state-dependent route. Delay and stochastic reaction problems have no single operator; see their linearise methods.

source
linearise(prob::DelayFiniteStateProblem, args...; kwargs...)

Not defined: with delayed terms the characteristic equation is transcendental, with infinitely many roots, so there is no matrix whose spectrum gives the stability. Expand each delay into a chain of stages (a linear chain trick) and linearise the resulting FiniteStateGeneratorProblem, or use simulation-based measures of recovery.

source
linearise(prob::FiniteStateReactionProblem, args...; kwargs...)

Not defined: a reaction problem is a stochastic jump process with no single operator. Its mean follows the deterministic generator problem with the same rates; linearise that, or use trajectory-based measures (recovery times, variability) on simulated realisations.

FiniteStatePopulationDynamics.scheduled_event — Function
scheduled_event(times, affect!; save_positions = (true, true))

Build a SciMLBase.DiscreteCallback that fires whenever the solver passes through (or exactly hits) any time in times. affect!(integrator) is called at each such time. times may be a scalar or an iterable of times.

The callback uses tstops semantics — callers should also pass tstops = times to solve to guarantee each event time is exactly visited.

source
FiniteStatePopulationDynamics.periodic_event — Function
periodic_event(period, affect!; t0 = 0.0, save_positions = (true, true))

Build a SciMLBase.DiscreteCallback that fires on a regular schedule t0, t0+period, t0+2*period, .... Callers should pass matching tstops to solve so the events are not skipped by adaptive time stepping.
